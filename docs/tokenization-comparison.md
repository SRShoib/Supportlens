# Tokenization comparison: spaCy vs BPE vs WordPiece

Generated by `scripts/compare_tokenization.py` from `tests/fixtures/gnarly_examples.json` (M1 deliverable, SPEC §4). Every example is run through `ml.data.cleaning.clean_text` first — these are the strings a model actually sees, not the raw tweets.

Four tokenizers compared: spaCy `en_core_web_sm` (rule-based + statistical), RoBERTa BPE **without** our mask tokens registered, RoBERTa BPE **with** `ml.data.masking.additional_special_tokens()` registered, and BERT WordPiece (also with special tokens registered).

## Analysis

**Mask-token survival.** In example 1, naive BPE fragments `<URL>` into 3 subword pieces (`<`, `URL`, `>`); registering `additional_special_tokens()` collapses it to the single atomic token `<URL>` — this is why M3's tokenizer setup must call `add_special_tokens()` with that exact list before fine-tuning, or the model trains on fragments of our own mask vocabulary instead of a stable token. WordPiece shows the same fix cleanly; RoBERTa's byte-level pre-tokenizer occasionally emits a stray leading-space token immediately before an added token (see the `Ġ` before `<URL>` in example 1, and before both `<USER>` in example 15) that WordPiece does not — a real quirk of byte-level BPE, not a bug in our cleaning. Notably, spaCy's tokenizer *also* has no built-in notion of our mask tokens and splits them into `<`, `USER`/`URL`/etc., `>` just like naive BPE (example 1) — a from-scratch spaCy pipeline would need its own `tokenizer.add_special_case()` rules to treat them atomically.

**`<EMOJI:...>` tokens never survive.** Unlike the four fixed mask tokens, emoji tokens are open-vocabulary (`<EMOJI:red_heart>`, `<EMOJI:thumbs_up_medium_skin_tone>`, ...) and cannot be pre-registered as a finite special-token list. Every tokenizer here fragments them into `<`, `EMOJI`, `:`, the name pieces, and `>` (examples 4, 11, 20). If a future module needs emoji as a single semantic unit, that requires a dedicated pre-tokenizer step, not `additional_special_tokens`.

**Hashtags** (examples 6, 20) split at the `#` under all three tokenizers — spaCy's rule-based tokenizer treats `#` as its own punctuation token just like BPE/WordPiece do (`#`, `fail`), so none of the three preserve `#fail` as a single unit. If topic/keyword features ever need whole hashtags (a plausible M7 input), that's a custom pre-tokenization rule for every tokenizer here, not something any of them do out of the box.

**Elongations** (example 3, "sooooooo") are already capped to 3 repeats by `collapse_repeats` before tokenization, so none of the tokenizers see the original 7-character run — this is deliberate cleaning-pipeline behavior, not a tokenizer difference, but it's why the elongation doesn't blow up subword counts here.

**Contractions** (example 8) — all three tokenizers split at the apostrophe, but disagree on where the word boundary falls: spaCy splits `can't` into `ca` + `n't` (a linguistic split matching the underlying "can" + "not"), BPE keeps `can` whole and splits off `'t`, and WordPiece splits furthest — `can` + `'` + `t` as three separate pieces. Neither is "more correct"; they're solving different problems (linguistic units vs. compression), but it means a feature computed by counting "words" gives three different answers depending on which tokenizer produced it.

**OOV subword explosion.** Misspellings and slang (examples 9, 12, 19 — "recieved", "damgaed", "lmaooo") inflate subword counts the most: BPE/WordPiece fall back to short, sometimes single-character pieces for text far from their pretraining distribution, while spaCy still emits one token per whitespace-delimited word regardless of spelling. This is the concrete evidence for why a subword vocabulary tolerates messy real-world text better than a fixed word-level vocabulary would: a classical bag-of-words pipeline treats "recieved" as pure out-of-vocabulary noise, while BPE/WordPiece decompose it into pieces seen during pretraining and still produce a usable representation.

**Token-count inflation**, averaged across all 20 examples: BPE without specials runs 1.61x spaCy's token count, dropping to 1.56x once mask tokens are registered; WordPiece lands at 1.55x. Registering the mask vocabulary is a measurable, not just cosmetic, difference in sequence length — and sequence length is exactly what M3's latency budget (SPEC §3) has to pay for.
